The problem

Calibration-free 4-bit DiTs usually collapse

Methods that reach W4A4 on diffusion models rely on calibration: smoothing factors, clipping ranges or low-rank corrections fitted to prompts run through one specific checkpoint. Repeat it for every model, fine-tune and bit-width.

Without calibration, plain round-to-nearest and HQQ produce noise on PixArt-Σ and lose the prompt on FLUX.1-schnell and SANA-1.6B. CentriQ stays close to the 16-bit output on all three.

The insight

A hidden mean survives the rotation

Adaptive LayerNorm shifts every token by a conditioning-dependent offset, so each activation carries a per-token mean. Hadamard rotation, the standard data-free tool for spreading outliers, cannot spread this mean at DiT widths (1152, 2240, 3072). It piles it onto one coordinate, and that spike sets the 4-bit step for the whole token.

The method

Center, rotate, restore exactly

Because a linear layer is linear, the mean can be taken out before quantization and added back afterwards with no error.

1

Center each token

Subtract the per-token mean $\bar{x}_t$, at $O(n)$ cost, in the same kernel launch that computes the scale.

2

Rotate & quantize

A fixed factorized Hadamard rotation mixes the centered token. One closed-form scale per token, shared clip settings across all models.

3

Restore the mean

The mean re-enters as an exact full-precision rank-1 term $\bar{x}_t\,\mathbf{w}_{\mathbf{1}}$, fused into the GEMM epilogue.

$$\mathbf{y}_t \;=\; \textcolor{#1673b8}{\widehat{W}_{R}\;Q_a\!\big(HP\,(\mathbf{x}_t-\bar{x}_t\mathbf{1})\big)} \;+\; \textcolor{#e2601b}{\bar{x}_t\,W\mathbf{1}} \;+\; \mathbf{b}$$
